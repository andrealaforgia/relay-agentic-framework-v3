**On the six departures.** I accept **1**, behaviour before project. `submit_review`, `_block` and settlement serialise on the behaviour row. I found no reversed lock order among those paths. I accept **2**, counters in place of generations, for this in-process driver where each send settles its own result once, subject to 2B-1.6 and 2B-1.8. I accept **3**, reopening red on an observed runtime change. The receipt should identify a runtime change explicitly because `BASE_MOVED` tells the caller that trunk changed. I challenge **4**, descendant-as-convergence, for 2B-1.3. I accept **5**, recording a stale-base decision as a batch for request replay. I accept **6**, sharing retry between rejected and conflicted intents, subject to 2B-1.7. Human abandonment after a *settled* rejection remains deferred scope. [Runtime-change receipt](/Users/andrealaforgia/dev/relay-v3/src/relay/application/integration.py:384)

**On lease failure during checks:** I favour reopening red automatically once a fresh, authoritative read establishes a strict advance from the expected base, fences every old send, and shows the candidate still requires work. A lease rejection alone is insufficient. An exact candidate means convergence. An unchanged base with a definitive hook refusal should retain the human retry gate. An uncertain reading keeps the intent outstanding.

**On one unanswered send followed by a refusal:** keep the slot. A permanent hook refusal today cannot establish what happened to a send that entered the remote earlier. A confirmed forward move on the same protected ref, or a remote fence that makes the earlier send incapable of landing, resolves that uncertainty. Record the intent ID, expected head, candidate and refusal together so the operator can see why the project is waiting.
